Restate your last message: plain words, and **short**. Keep only what the reader needs to
act or decide. Cut the rest.

- **Lead with the answer.** First sentence carries the point. No wind-up, no restating the
  question, no "as I mentioned".
- **Aim for a few sentences.** A short list is fine when there really are separate items.
  Tables, headings and section breaks usually mean you are still explaining rather than
  answering — drop them unless the content is genuinely a set of parallel items.
- **One level of detail.** Give the conclusion, not the trail that led to it. Keep a file
  path, number, or name only when the reader would have to go looking for it otherwise.
- **No jargon.** Say the thing in the words a colleague outside the codebase would use.
- **Drop the caveats** unless one changes what the reader would do.

Never sacrifice accuracy for brevity. If something is uncertain, or you were wrong, say so
in the fewest words that stay honest — do not let a shorter answer become a more confident
one than the evidence supports. Cutting words is the goal; cutting the truth is not.
